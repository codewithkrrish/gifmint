How it works

  1. Choose a video from your device. It is never uploaded anywhere.
  2. Trim the exact segment you want with the sliders or the quick-set buttons.
  3. Pick a frame rate, width and quality, then preview the trimmed segment looped.
  4. Press Convert to GIF, watch the progress, then play back and download the result.

Honest notes

Keep clips short. Clips under about 15 seconds convert fast and stay small. Longer clips still work, but encoding slows down and the tab uses much more memory — a warning appears above the Convert button when a clip crosses that line.

GIFs are big for what they are. GIF compression is old and inefficient, so a GIF can end up larger than the video it came from. Narrower widths and lower frame rates are the two levers that actually shrink the file; the comparison under your finished GIF shows the honest result.

Encoding runs in your browser. GIFMint uses gif.js (version 0.2.0, loaded from jsDelivr) with a background worker. If that script cannot load — for example, with no connection — conversion is unavailable and the page says so; your video still never leaves the device.

Input support varies by browser. MP4 and WebM cover nearly everything, but whether a particular video decodes depends on your browser's codecs, not on GIFMint. If a file loads but shows no picture, try the same clip in another browser or re-export it as MP4.

Keep the tab open while converting. Capturing frames means seeking through your video one frame at a time; switching away can slow it down, and closing the tab loses the unfinished GIF. Finished GIFs are yours to download — nothing is stored after you leave.

No audio is carried into a GIF — the format has no sound track, so the result is always silent.
